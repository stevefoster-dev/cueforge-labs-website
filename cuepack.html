02

Decide what travels

The budget decision becomes a buildable gear system

Vendor lines, production budgets and case-ready gear live in the same operating record. CuePack can expose the trade-off and prepare the next step; the winner and the final package remain human decisions.

Job — compare the package against the plan

Three lighting categories, two vendors, one visible budget impact

The comparison view puts coverage, weekly totals and the production budget in one decision surface. Each vendor can win individual lines, but no quote is silently accepted and no undecided package turns itself into inventory.

  • Both vendors cover all three compared categories
  • Per-line best values and weekly totals remain visible together
  • Budget impact reports the remaining planning basis for each option
  • Select Winner controls keep commercial responsibility with the production

Boundary These are rounded internal-production fixture amounts, not CuePack pricing, an approved purchase order or a financial forecast.

Job — turn decided items into physical units

Gear records become cases, racks and trunks with real limits

Once a winner is resolved, CuePack's explicit Push to Pack path can create canonical gear records without duplicating an item already materialised. The build workspace then assigns that gear into dimensioned containers and keeps unresolved items in plain sight.

  • Owned and vendor-supplied gear can share the same package workspace
  • Case type, dimensions, weight, rack units and fill remain editable
  • Unassigned items stay flagged until a real container is chosen
  • Case manifests create physical pack-ready units for movement planning

Boundary The build frame is a separate exact-runtime fixture from the quote frame. It demonstrates the next product surface, not a claim that the pictured quote was purchased.

03

One production record

From the first date to the first call

CuePack is the operating spine between planning, physical logistics and the day itself. Each stage narrows the same production record instead of beginning another spreadsheet.

  1. 01

    Import

    Bring in the schedule and map its date, city and venue columns.

  2. 02

    Match

    Resolve each stop against the venue database and review every new place.

  3. 03

    Decide

    Compare vendor lines against the production budget and choose deliberately.

  4. 04

    Build

    Materialise decided gear, case it, group it and keep exceptions visible.

  5. 05

    Move

    Choose truck, air or sea and review the editable pack before release.

  6. 06

    Run

    Publish the calls and show each role only the operating view it needs.

Built for

  • Production managers carrying a tour from advance through show day
  • Rental and shop teams turning decided gear into repeatable case packs
  • Logistics leads comparing truck, air and sea movement
  • PA and crew teams working from role-bounded calls and venue facts

Outside its authority

  • It does not purchase a vendor package or approve a budget automatically
  • It is not a freight broker, carrier booking system or customs filing service
  • It does not replace a weight certificate, axle check or legal load inspection
  • LED surface design belongs in CuePatch; settlement stays with finance tools

05

Run the day

The same call, two operating views

Production sees the exceptions that need intervention. Crew sees the call, venue facts and status action required of them. Role-based views are part of the same system, not a second app.

Both field frames were exercised through CuePack's current role paths against one synthetic show-day fixture. No artist, customer, real venue, crew identity, email, host, network address or production service appears in the delivered pixels.

06

Availability

The commercial model is decided. The numbers are not.

In Development — Private Workflow Validation

How CuePack will be sold

Productions are seasonal, so CuePack is not sold as a flat per-seat subscription. The approved commercial architecture has four parts, and the exact amounts for each are still being set. No price is quoted until they are.

  • Account membership. The workspace, the gear and case library, and the operational history stay alive whether or not a production is currently out.
  • Active production packs. You pay for the productions that are actually running, not for a permanent seat count.
  • Scale add-ons. Larger truck-scale movements are priced above the standard production pack.
  • Standby and archive. A reduced state that keeps a dark production's history and templates available for the next leg.

Onboarding, implementation and support are quoted separately from the software. There is no public checkout, and CuePack is not billed automatically today.
