02

In the tech

The room is dark, both hands are busy, and the note still lands on the right beat

CueRoll assumes you have no spare hand to hunt for a timestamp. It reads the clock the show is already running, resolves it to the song everyone else is naming, and stamps the note for you.

Job — never guess the time again

Exact timecode, resolved to the exact song

A native decoder reads Linear Timecode from the audio interface, and the setlist turns that number into the song title, the act, and the range it sits inside. The number on screen is the number the department will search for tomorrow.

  • LTC decoded from any audio input, including virtual soundcards
  • Configurable timecode input channel for multichannel interfaces
  • Automatic song detection from the setlist's timecode ranges
  • Mismatch warning when incoming timecode drifts from the expected range

Boundary CueRoll does not generate timecode. It reads the show's existing clock and never drives consoles, media servers or playback.

Job — keep up with the room

One row per department, one key to reach it

Each department gets its own field, and each field has a single-key shortcut. Press the letter, type, press Enter — the song and timecode are attached at the moment of the note, not at the moment you got round to filing it.

  • Separate note rows for lighting, video, cameras, audio and general
  • Single-key focus per department and tab cycling between them
  • Enter submits; timecode is captured at the instant of the note
  • Follow-TC and manual modes for when the clock is not running
  • Per-song note view so a song can be reviewed as a unit

Boundary Live Link is a local-network tool for the room you are in, not a hosted collaboration service.

03

The safe loop

Lock timecode, take the note, review before anything leaves

Notes are captured locally first and published deliberately. Nothing is pushed to a shared destination because you typed it fast in the dark.

  1. 01

    Chase timecode

    Decode LTC from the audio input the show is already feeding you, on the channel you select.

  2. 02

    Capture

    Hit the department key, type, and submit — the song and timecode are stamped for you.

  3. 03

    Collect from the room

    Crew on the local network add their own notes from a phone or laptop, attributed by name.

  4. 04

    Review

    Work the drafts, tidy the wording, and decide what is actually worth publishing to the department.

  5. 05

    Publish

    Send the approved set to the shared Notion database the production already uses.

Built for

  • Lighting programmers and designers working a tech or dress
  • Video engineers logging content and sync issues against the timeline
  • Production and stage managers running notes sessions
  • Music directors tracking timing and arrangement changes across rehearsals

Outside its authority

  • It does not generate timecode — it reads the show's existing clock
  • It does not control consoles, media servers or playback
  • Live Link is a local-network tool, not a hosted collaboration service
  • Programming cues from timecode is CueMaker's job, not CueRoll's
