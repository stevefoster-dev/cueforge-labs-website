02

Evidence first

Nothing reaches the invoice until you accept it

Most invoicing tools start with an empty form and ask you to remember the agreement. InvoicePack starts with the agreement, proposes each fact with the sentence it came from, and changes the draft only when you apply what you accepted. Every image on this page is cropped from the same exact synthetic local-vault runtime evidence.

Job — stop retyping the contract

Each proposed fact carries its own citation

Extracted facts arrive one at a time with a confidence weighting, the quoted sentence they came from and the page it sits on. You accept, edit or reject each one, and applying the accepted set is the only action that touches the invoice draft.

  • Billing address, client legal name, receipt requirements and invoice schedule proposed separately
  • Every proposal quoting its source sentence and page rather than asserting a conclusion
  • Accept, edit or reject per fact, with decisions saved before anything is applied
  • Contract attention flags raised deterministically with the clause they refer to

Boundary The review flags are prompts, not legal advice. The interface states it plainly: they are points to raise, not conclusions, and they do not replace a lawyer reading your agreement.

Job — get the money right

Rates you keep, edits that stay on one invoice

Work is billed from saved rate profiles and presets. Tapping a preset adds a line, and repeating it adds another line rather than quietly merging — and a rate you change on this invoice never rewrites the preset you saved.

  • Rate profiles and presets managed once, then reused per project
  • Line items with their own unit, quantity and rate, each one removable
  • Explicit switches for whether a line counts toward total time and whether it is taxable
  • The live document recomputing lines, subtotal and total time as the work changes

Boundary Arithmetic, persistence and export were exercised against synthetic projects during private validation. This is not an accounting, payroll or tax system, and it does not file anything on your behalf.

03

The safe loop

Ingest, review, apply, then export the packet

The loop keeps the source of truth and the paperwork in the same place, so what you send is traceable back to the sentence it came from.

  1. 01

    Ingest

    Paste an agreement or email thread, or add a PDF or message file. The raw source is preserved byte-for-byte inside the local project vault.

  2. 02

    Review the evidence

    Work through the proposed facts with their citations, accept or reject each one, and read the attention flags raised against the contract language.

  3. 03

    Apply to the draft

    Applying the accepted set is the only action that changes the invoice draft, so the draft never contains a fact nobody approved.

  4. 04

    Build the work

    Add billable lines from your rate profiles, set what counts toward total time, and watch the live document recompute as you go.

  5. 05

    Export and keep

    Export a work packet bundling the invoice with the stored documents and receipts for that project, and keep a copy in the project's own local output folder.

  6. 06

    Back up and lock

    Back up the vault, restore it, or lock it — all against a store that lives under your own user account with owner-only permissions.

Built for

  • Owner-operators who invoice against a signed agreement rather than a template
  • Anyone whose receipts, contract terms and invoice currently live in three separate places
  • Work where the client will ask what a line item was for, months later
  • People who would rather their contracts and receipts never left the machine

Stated limits

  • Raw agreements, receipts, tax and payment documents, PDFs and packet contents stay local by default
  • Only the minimum structured status is intended to flow back to Client Hub — that handoff is not built and accepted yet
  • Validation has been exercised with synthetic projects, never with real sensitive documents
  • There is no public distribution, no signed or notarised build claim, and no self-service release
  • The exercised build is a Mac-local application; no native Windows build is claimed

05

Status

A local-first candidate, honestly described

Private Validation — Local-First Candidate

InvoicePack is under private validation, not on public release.

The build shown here is a local-first candidate. Its evidence review, accepted facts, invoice drafting, arithmetic, local persistence, packet export and backup and restore have all been exercised end to end — against synthetic projects, on one machine, by a person watching every step.

What has not happened matters just as much: there is no public distribution path, no signed or notarised build claim, no self-service release, no published price, and no workflow that puts your sensitive documents in someone else's cloud. Nothing on this page should be read as an availability date.

  • Exercised in validation: source ingest with byte-for-byte preservation, cited fact review, applied-fact drafting, rate profiles and line arithmetic, local persistence, work-packet export, backup and restore, and the true-phone layout.
  • Not claimed: production readiness, self-service purchase, a distribution channel, a completed Client Hub handoff, or any use with real sensitive documents.
  • If this is your problem — invoicing against real agreements, with receipts you cannot afford to scatter — that is exactly the workflow being validated, and it is worth a conversation.
