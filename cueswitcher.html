The safe loop

Plan the show, rehearse the cut, follow it live

The shot sheet is the source of truth. The timeline projects it onto real time, and the show-mode view follows that timeline — hand-authored work is never overwritten by a projection.

  1. 01

    Plot the cameras

    Place the camera positions the show actually has, and name them the way the crew does.

  2. 02

    Build the shot sheet

    Work song by song and section by section, assigning framings per camera.

  3. 03

    Anchor to time

    Attach audio, capture the printed timecode, and let sections land at real positions.

  4. 04

    Rehearse

    Scrub the timeline, run the transport, and check the cut against the music.

  5. 05

    Run show mode

    Chase timecode and give every operator a countdown to their next shot.

Capabilities

A director's workspace, not a control surface replacement

Plan the coverage

Camera plot and shot sheet

The plan is built from the show's real camera positions, so the shot sheet columns mean something to the operators reading them.

  • Camera plot that defines the positions used everywhere else
  • Section-level shot sheet with per-camera framings
  • Per-cell framing overrides on top of a section default
  • Copy and paste sections with their shots and framings intact
  • Undo on shot-sheet edits

Put it on a clock

Setlist, timeline and countdowns

Songs get absolute positions from the timecode printed on their audio, so a countdown is a real number rather than an even division of a typed duration.

  • Setlist that appends rather than overwriting existing songs
  • Offline scan of embedded timecode at assignment time, without playing the track
  • Per-file timecode anchors, with a manual picker when alternates conflict
  • Timeline with drag-to-move, edge-drag resize and a cue inspector
  • Per-camera countdowns driven by actual cue positions

Follow the show

Timecode chase and show mode

Show mode is the cue-follow cockpit; chase is an input to it, never a lock on the operator.

  • Timecode chase from an audio device, a file or MIDI
  • Chase state shared across open views so every badge agrees
  • Manual seeks are never blocked by chase status
  • Scrub, play from a clicked position, and snap only where it helps

Drive what is supported

Macros, adapters and handoff

The macro runtime is switcher-agnostic; each supported system has its own adapter, and nothing is claimed for hardware that has not been proven.

  • Neutral macro model exported per switcher type
  • Adapters for ATEM, Ross, vMix and OBS
  • Cue-driven macro firing when the playhead crosses a cue
  • Show-control messaging for common media-server and playback systems
  • Portable show export and import, plus an operator shot-sheet PDF

Field fit

Who it is for — and where it stops

Built for

  • Directors planning and rehearsing a cut for a music or corporate show
  • Camera operators who need a countdown to their next framing
  • Technical directors preparing macros and layouts before doors
  • Video departments that want the plan and the run in one document

Outside its authority

  • Switcher support is limited to the adapters listed above; other systems are not claimed
  • Camera-control adapters for further manufacturers are not available
  • It does not replace the physical switcher, the TD, or the director's judgement
  • Desktop builds are unsigned and are not a customer distribution

Availability

In development, no published price

In Development — Private Validation

CueSwitcher is not on public sale.

CueSwitcher is being validated privately against real shows. Its commercial model is unresolved — whether the application itself is a one-time purchase or an annual licence, and whether additional protocol adapters are priced separately, has not been decided. No amount is quoted.

Signing and notarisation for macOS and Windows remain open, so there is no public installer today.
